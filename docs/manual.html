OKTODUO manual · version 0.1 · for the M-VAVE FM-1

OKTODUO is the duo edition of OKTOWAVE, an alternative firmware for the M-VAVE FM-1 pocket synthesizer: Hügelton Instruments' Felucca 1.6.1 with two synthesis engines to choose from, its FM6 and WAVELET, the engines of the Wavelet Synthesizer by Pierre Arnaud GUYOT (OktoPierre), rewritten for the FM-1's processor: both of its engines (A, subtractive, and B, additive) and 108 of its presets, all of OktoPierre's TOP Sounds among them. Everything else is Felucca: four tracks, the sequencer, arpeggiator, chords, effects, USB audio and MIDI. This manual covers what OKTODUO adds; for the rest, Felucca's wiki is the reference.

The same sources make two other editions, each with its own installer, browser demo and manual: OKTOWAVE (all of Felucca's engines plus WAVELET) and OKTOGROOVE (WAVELET and the drum kits only).

This is the duo edition (branch oktoduo): two synths on one Felucca groovebox. It has Felucca's four tracks, 64-step sequencer (ratchets, chance, accents, slides, conditions), arpeggiator, chord keys, effects, USB audio and MIDI, and two synths to choose from for each track: FM6, Felucca's six-operator FM synth (32 algorithms, the DX-style voices and the voice bank), and WAVELET, the Wavelet Synthesizer's engines A and B (108 presets, OktoPierre's TOP Sounds first), plus DRUM, Felucca's kits, for the rhythm tracks. The other engines of Felucca (ANALOG, PHASE, LOFI, SAMPLE, VOICE, TRIO, WHEEL, GRAIN, PHYS, NOISE, SLICE) are not offered (their factory sounds are not in the PRESETS list, they are not on the EDIT keys); they stay in the firmware, so a project or a user preset saved by Felucca or OKTOWAVE that uses one still loads and sounds (a user preset saved on one of them stays in the PRESETS list; the ENG column then shows its real name and the EDIT keys show none lit). The editions with more engines are OKTOWAVE (all of them) and OKTOGROOVE (WAVELET and DRUM only). Section 12 has the details.

Version 0.1 has not been run on an FM-1 yet. It was built with the official toolchain and tested on computers only: Felucca's own test suite (the firmware's code against a simulated FM-1), the engine's standalone test against a floating-point reference, and the browser build of the whole firmware. Installing a custom firmware is at your own risk. Back up your songs first (the installer offers it), and keep the way back: M-VAVE's updater or the installer's Return to official V15. If an install fails and the FM-1 no longer starts, recovery needs FM-1-transporter.

1. Install

  1. Connect the FM-1 to the computer with a USB cable and switch it on.
  2. Open the web installer (the page of this edition) in Chrome or Edge (Web MIDI is needed: recent Firefox asks for a permission, Safari does not have it).
  3. Press Install and follow the page. The FM-1 restarts into OKTODUO; the menu shows OKTODUO v0.1.

From the command line, with Python 3 and pip3 install mido python-rtmidi: python3 tools/fm1_install.py oktoduo-0.1.fwsc (the package is in the releases, under the tag duo-v0.1).

OKTODUO is a Felucca package: it installs over Felucca, the official firmware or another custom firmware the same way, and Felucca's installer, editor and backup tools work with it. Songs and user presets saved by Felucca 1.6 load as they are; a song or a user preset that uses a WAVELET part is not loaded by plain Felucca (it shows the slot as empty: the engine is not there), and the songs this firmware saves from its power-on tracks do use WAVELET.

2. Try it in the browser

The page opens on a window with four choices: Run the demo (the four tracks play and the lead walks through the FM6 sounds), Play the synth (switch the FM-1 on and play), Install on an FM-1 (the installer) and User manual. The firmware in your browser: the same code, compiled to WebAssembly, with the FM-1's screen, buttons, knobs and keys drawn on the page (after X0X's and Felucca's browser builds). Click the keys or use the computer keyboard. It keeps its saves in the browser. Not there: USB, MIDI and the user sample slots.

3. Quick start

At power-on the four tracks are ready to play together, all in C minor: track 1 the FM bass FM BASS (FM6) with the pattern OKTOBASS, track 2 the pad WARM PAD (WAVELET, a TOP Sound) with OKTOCHRD, track 3 the FM electric piano TINE EP (FM6) with OKTOLEAD, track 4 the DRUM kit with BEAT. The pad and the electric piano play MONO (one voice each, legato; the VOICE page changes it), which leaves the shared budget of eight voices room for the bass. Press PLAY to hear them; PLAY again stops. The keys play the selected track, so you can play over the groove or change any sound while it runs. The new patterns are in SAVE › PHRASES for any track.

  • Choose a sound: turn the PRESETS knob. In the PRESETS page (SAVE, one tap) the KNOB 2 steps through the engines (the ENG column); the PRESETS knob steps through the sounds.
  • Choose the track: the ALGORITHM knob steps through the four tracks (on every page). The SELECT knob is the tempo.
  • The four knobs on HOME (HOME): for a WAVELET sound they are CUT (the cutoff), RES (resonance), SPRS (sparsity; TILT in engine B) and REL (release).
  • All eight engine parameters: EDIT opens the page ATOMS (WAVE, WVLT, PHAS or WDTH, SPRS); EDIT again opens SCALE (CUT, RES, FLT, WNDW). Each knob is one parameter. With WAVE on one of the six engine-B patterns (RAMP .. RAND) the knobs read BARS and TILT instead of PHAS and SPRS, and FADE instead of WNDW (section 5).
  • Envelope, LFO, voices, effects: the ENV, LFO, EDIT (VOICE pages) and FX buttons, as for every Felucca engine (section 6).
  • Save: SAVE (USER page) stores the sound in one of 32 user slots; projects keep the four tracks, their sounds and patterns.

Turning WAVE, WVLT, PHAS/WDTH or SPRS (engine A) or WAVE or WVLT (engine B) rebuilds the sound's wave table: the new sound fades in over about 12 ms once the build is done (a few to about 50 ms, longer with DB20 than with HAAR; the old sound plays meanwhile). Everything else, BARS, TILT and FADE included, acts at once and rebuilds nothing.

4. How the WAVELET engine works

A classic subtractive synth filters an oscillator by frequency. WAVELET filters it by scale. One cycle of the source wave (512 samples) is taken apart by a periodic orthogonal discrete wavelet transform into 511 atoms grouped in nine bands: band 1 holds one atom as long as the cycle (the fundamental), band 2 two atoms half as long (around the second harmonic), band 3 four atoms, and so on to band 9, 256 atoms of two samples (harmonics 128 to 256). Which atom shapes are used is the wavelet: Haar atoms are square steps, Daubechies 2 is rough and short, Daubechies 20 is long and smooth, nearly a sine, with symlets and coiflets in between. Every band is turned back into a wave of its own (a layer); the nine layers add up to the source again.

A voice reads the nine layers at its pitch and adds them with one gain each. The gains come from the scale filter: a cutoff in bands, a slope in dB per band, a resonance peak at the cutoff. Because the bands are tied to the cycle, not to a frequency in hertz, the timbre is the same on every key (the VST's key tracking at 100 %). SPARSITY keeps only the largest atoms before the layers are made: the wave loses its small details first, then its shape, down to one atom. PHASE shifts the source inside the cycle before the transform: the transform is not shift-invariant, so the same wave gives other atoms, and another timbre. The window (WNDW) cross-fades, inside a raised-cosine bump that covers half the cycle, between the dry cycle and the filtered one; SHAPE (the envelope's and the LFO's SHP amounts) moves the bump round the cycle.

Engine B, the additive one, is the same engine with its atoms set by a pattern instead of a source wave (WAVE 6 to 11: RAMP, MIRR, ALT, WALS, IMPL, RAND): every atom of a band at the same level (RAMP), the first half up and the second half down (MIRR), up or down by band (ALT), alternating by atom (WALS), the first atom of each band only (IMPL), random (RAND). Each band's layer is scaled to the same peak; what shapes the sound is the gain of each band: a registration (BARS: one of 16 sets of nine levels, like an organ's drawbars), a tilt (TILT: how much each band is below the one under it) and a scale envelope that swells with the amp attack and, with FADE, lets every band fall toward the level of SUS, the faster the higher the band (the top band in the time FADE sets, the lowest about six times slower). The cutoff, the resonance and the filter type act on the bands as in engine A. There is no sparsity, no window and no phase in engine B.

Everything runs in fixed point, with no floating point: the tables are 16-bit, the gains 10-bit fractions, the transforms 20-bit samples with 28-bit coefficients. A WAVELET voice costs about twice an ANALOG voice (measured on a computer, 1.6 to 2.3 times; not yet measured on the FM-1), a little more while a new table fades in. So that the FM-1 is never asked for more than for ANALOG's eight voices, a WAVELET part plays at most three voices (POLY and UNISON) and a WAVELET voice counts as two voices of the shared budget of eight (four WAVELET voices in all, with or without other tracks: when the budget is full the oldest released voice goes first, else the oldest held one, never the lowest held note). Bands that are filtered out or beyond the pitch's limit are skipped in the sum. The two cores share the parts as for every engine; the table builds run on the first core, a slice at a time.

ATOMS · 2WVLTHAAR DB2 DB4 DB8 SYM8 COIF3 DB20The wavelet: the atoms' shape. HAAR = steps (chip-like, bright); DB4 (the default) and DB8 = the all-rounders; SYM8, COIF3 = smoother, more symmetric; DB20 = nearly sines (the filter then works almost like a harmonic filter).
ATOMS · 3PHAS0 .. 100 %The source's phase: where the cycle starts, in 128ths of a cycle. 0 is the Wavelet Synthesizer's sound; other values give other atoms, often a brighter or hollower version of the same wave. Hear it best with HAAR or DB2.
ATOMS · 3WDTH0 .. 100 %On PLS and TRI the knob is the pulse width / the triangle's rising part, 2 to 98 % of the cycle (50 % = square / symmetric triangle). These two waves have no phase knob.
ATOMS · 4SPRS0 .. 100 %Sparsity: 0 keeps every atom; the knob throws away the smallest ones, more and more (K = N1 − s atoms of the N there are), down to a single atom at 100 %. Thins and simplifies the wave; with NOIS it makes pitched, glassy textures.
SCALE · 1CUT0 .. 127The cutoff, in bands: 127 is band 12, fully open; 64 is band 6 (harmonics 16 to 32); 0 is below the fundamental. The envelope's and LFO's FLT amounts move it by up to ±12 bands.
SCALE · 2RES0 .. 100 %Resonance: a boost of up to 24 dB of the band at the cutoff (and a little of its neighbours).
SCALE · 3FLTLP12 .. LP96, HP12 .. HP96, BP12 .. BP96The filter's type and slope: low-pass, high-pass or band-pass, 12 18 24 36 48 or 96 dB per band (24 = the VST's default). The slope is per band, that is per octave of atoms: LP24 is gentle, LP96 is a wall.
SCALE · 4WNDW0 .. 100 %The window's depth: 0 = the filtered sound everywhere; 100 % = the dry cycle outside the window and the filtered one inside it. In between, a mix. The window is half a cycle wide; its place is SHAPE (section 6). Engine A only.
ATOMS · 3BARS0 .. 127Engine B. The registration: one of 16 sets of nine levels, BARS / 8. 0 = all bands at the same level (the buzziest), then the sets the presets use: organ-like (a few strong low bands), flute (the first three bands), choir (all bands), bell (gaps) and so on.
ATOMS · 4TILT0 .. 100 %Engine B. How much each band is below the one under it: 0 = none, 100 % = 24 dB per band (only the fundamental is left).
SCALE · 4FADE0 .. 100 %Engine B. 0 = no fade. Higher: every band falls toward the level of SUS (the ENV page), the faster the higher the band (at 100 % the top band in a millisecond, the lowest about six times slower; the way a xylophone or a marimba dies). Back to 0 the bands come back to full. The sound swells over the amp ATK first.

The values 0 .. 127 of the knobs are what Felucca's editor and the SysEx protocol see (engine values E0 .. E7); the screen shows them as above.

6. Envelope, LFO, voices, effects

These are Felucca's, the same for every engine; what matters for WAVELET:

  • ENV: the ADSR of the voice's level. Its second page, ENV DEST: FLT moves the cutoff with the envelope (−64 .. +63 = −12 .. +12 bands: the classic filter sweep of a pluck or a brass sound); SHP moves the window's place with the envelope; PIT the pitch.
  • LFO: rate, wave, phase, fade; LFO DEST: FLT sweeps the cutoff (wobbles, auto-wah), SHP sweeps the window round the cycle (a phaser-like motion when WNDW is up), PIT vibrato, AMP tremolo. The MOD page's matrix (velocity, aftertouch, random, …) reaches the same destinations.
  • VOICE pages (EDIT, after the engine pages): POLY / MONO / LEGATO / UNISON, glide, detune, pan, spread. UNISON with DETUNE is what the Wavelet Synthesizer's UNISON 2 .. 4 did; the presets that used it are marked below.
  • FX: the sends to distortion, chorus, delay and reverb, and the track's INSERT. The presets carry their drive, chorus, delay and reverb amounts from the VST.
  • The scale and chord keys (SCL), the arpeggiator (ARP) and the sequencer (SEQ) play a WAVELET track like any other.

7. Differences from the Wavelet Synthesizer

The FM-1 has a small integer processor and no floating point, so the engine is a reduced version of the VST / web app (beta 0.7, engines A and B). What it keeps: the source waves, the seven wavelets, the atoms and bands, sparsity, the source phase, the scale filter (types, slope, resonance, envelope and LFO to the cutoff), the window and its position. What is reduced or left out:

2048-sample cycle, 11 bands, 11 band-limited mip levels per layer512-sample cycle, 9 bands (harmonics 1 .. 256; the VST's bands 10 and 11 would be above 10 kHz on any note above 40 Hz). Anti-aliasing is much weaker than in the VST. There are no mip levels: a band whose harmonics would pass Nyquist is faded out, one octave coarse, and the dry cycle under the window is gated the same way. The wavelet layers themselves are not band-limited, so edgy waves (SAW and PLS with HAAR or DB2) alias audibly on high notes: measured on a computer, the energy off the harmonics is about 30 dB under the harmonics at C3 and 10 to 16 dB under at C6 to C8 (ANALOG's saw: 48 down to 34 dB). 9. Sound design tips

  • Start from the wavelet. The same saw through HAAR, DB4 and DB20 is three instruments: steps, a textured saw, a smooth one. Then CUT and RES.
  • Sparsity is a second filter. Low values clean the wave (it drops the tiny atoms); 40 .. 70 % turns any wave into a lighter, hollow version of itself; high values leave a few atoms: a pitched click, a formant. With NOIS, sparsity + a resonant band-pass = glassy, bell-like noises.
  • Phase is cheap variation. Two tracks with the same preset and different PHAS values do not sound alike: good for a two-layer pad.
  • Engine B for organs, choirs and bells. Start from a registration (BARS), then TILT: flat is buzzy, −10 to −25 % is an organ, more is a flute; RAMP is the bright one, MIRR a hollow one (even bands cancel), IMPL a pulse train, RAND a noisy, metallic one. FADE with a low cutoff makes plucks and bells.
  • The window as a phaser. WNDW 100 %, LFO DEST SHP at half, a slow LFO: the filtered part of the cycle sweeps round, a slow phase-like motion that follows the pitch.
  • Brass and plucks: ENV DEST FLT positive with a quick decay; pads: slow attack, DB8 or SYM8, chorus; basses: MONO, HAAR or DB2, LP24 with a little RES.
  • The filter is in bands, not hertz: a cutoff of 64 lets harmonics 1 .. ~24 through on every key. For a bass with a fixed-frequency feel, play it low with a low CUT.

10. Build and test it yourself

The power-on tracks and their patterns differ from Felucca's: that is this firmware's own (a project saved by Felucca, and the host tests, still start from Felucca's defaults). Favourites: a WAVELET preset is starred like any other (FAV on the PRESETS page, the editor). Felucca's favourites table has no row left for a fifteenth engine, so WAVELET's stars are bits in bytes 8 to 31 of the row of the retired DIGITAL engine. A settings backup restored on plain Felucca loses them and nothing else.

The source is on GitHub. The firmware builds like Felucca (BUILDING.md: JieLi's pi32v2 clang 4.0.1, Linux x86-64; three files of the Apache-2.0 AC79 SDK; Python with Pillow and fontTools). The repository's GitHub Actions workflow does all of it on a Linux runner and publishes the package, the test results, the browser build and this manual.

The engine alone needs only a C compiler and Node.js:

mkdir -p build/host
python3 tools/gen_tables.py build/host/felucca_tables.h
cc -O2 -std=gnu99 -I build/host -o build/host/wavelet_test tests/wavelet_test.c -lm
build/host/wavelet_test build/host     # every wave x wavelet, the presets (writes preset_NN.wav), the cross-fade, the anti-aliasing
node tools/wavelet_ref.js build/host   # the fixed-point tables against the floating-point reference

12. The duo edition

  • The idea: a groovebox with two different synths side by side: the FM synth for bells, electric pianos, basses and brass, the WAVELET engine for pads, strings, organs, leads and textures, each track on whichever it needs, a track of drums on the kits. The idea of an FM synth with a second synth on the FM-1 is also that of FM-1+VA by Baud Girl, which adds virtual analog synth engines and a 64-step sequencer, as Synth Anatomy and its vendor describe it. When this edition was written no source of it was published that we could find (the community directory fm1-editor.com lists it as "Not on GitHub"; Synth Anatomy mentions a planned open-source release), so OKTODUO is built on Felucca, whose source is open. It does not use any of Baud Girl's code, designs or assets: it only shares the idea of two synths. Baud Girl's second synth is virtual analog; here the second synth is WAVELET.   • What changes from Felucca: the engine list (PRESETS knob 2 / ENG column and the EDIT layer's white keys: FM6, WAVELET, DRUM, so INIT and CLEAN INIT are the fourth and fifth white keys) and the power-on tracks. Tracks 2 to 4 of a project saved in Felucca's first format (FUN1) also start as the WAVELET pad, the FM6 electric piano and the DRUM kit, with no pattern. At power-on the four tracks play together after a press of PLAY, in C minor: track 1 the FM bass FM BASS (pattern OKTOBASS), track 2 the pad WARM PAD of WAVELET (OKTOCHRD), track 3 the FM electric piano TINE EP (OKTOLEAD), track 4 the drum kit (BEAT). Nothing else changes: the storage formats, the editor protocol, USB and MIDI are Felucca's byte for byte (WAVELET's stars: section 10).
  • The WAVELET engine is as described above (sections 4 to 8). A WAVELET part plays at most three voices, and a WAVELET voice counts as two of the eight voices the four tracks share (so four WAVELET voices in all, the oldest released voice going first when a note needs room), so that the FM-1 is never asked for more than it does for Felucca's eight ANALOG voices. The FM6 engine's voices count one each.
  • Old projects: a track left on an engine this edition does not offer plays as before. The ENG column shows its real name, the engine knob goes to FM6 (clockwise) or DRUM (counter-clockwise), and the PRESETS knob to the first sound of the list (clockwise) or the last one. Felucca's RESTORE LAST brings the previous session back at the first power-on after Felucca or OKTOWAVE, so the first track you meet can be such a one. The user presets saved on a hidden engine stay in the PRESETS list.
  • The editor (webapp/editor/) is Felucca's: it lists all fifteen engines, in its PRESETS mirror too, and can select any of them. Only the device's own pages offer FM6, WAVELET and DRUM alone.
  • Install and try: the same package format and installer as the other editions (projects saved with one load in the others, except that plain Felucca does not load a project that uses WAVELET, section 1; the package is oktoduo-X.Y.fwsc, released under the tag duo-vX.Y); the version string reads OKTODUO v0.1. Try it in the browser: the page of this edition under /duo/ on the project's site.
